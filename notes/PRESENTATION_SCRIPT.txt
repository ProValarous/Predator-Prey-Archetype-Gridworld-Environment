SECTION 8 — NEW WORK: ACTORCRITIC BASELINE (FIRST LOOK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"We've added a second baseline alongside DQN: ActorCritic, a one-step online
actor-critic — Sutton and Barto's Algorithm 13.5. Where DQN is off-policy with a
replay buffer and a target network, this is fully on-policy: one network per
agent with a shared trunk feeding a policy head and a value head, and every
single environment step immediately triggers one gradient update from that
step's TD error. No replay buffer, no target network, and no epsilon schedule —
the stochastic policy itself provides the exploration.

It's wired in exactly like every other baseline: registered in the algorithm
registry, driven by its own YAML config, with 21 new tests, and the full suite
of 344 tests still passes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9 — FIRST VERIFICATION RUN (BEFORE A KNOWN FIX)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Before reporting anything as a real result, we wanted to actually watch it train
and check the numbers made sense — not just that the code runs without error.
We ran it on the shared 3-predator-vs-3-prey config: 10x10 grid, 20% obstacles,
predator speed 1, prey speed 3, 1000 episodes.

The predator showed a U-shape similar to the old DQN speed sweep — reward
improves from Q1 to Q3, then drops back down by Q4. But we found something that
made us hold off calling that confirmed: the critic's loss was enormous —
hundreds of thousands for the predators — and it was not shrinking over the
full 1000 episodes.

We tracked this down: the critic was using plain squared-error loss, but DQN
deliberately uses Huber loss instead, specifically because it's robust to large
TD errors. This environment's rewards accumulate into the thousands per
episode, so squared error on targets that large produces huge, destabilizing
gradients — and since the policy and value heads share the same trunk, that
instability could be dragging down the policy's learning too."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10 — AFTER THE HUBER LOSS FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"We made the fix and re-ran the identical config. The loss fix worked exactly as
expected: predator loss dropped from the hundreds of thousands down to the low
hundreds, prey loss from about 14,800 down to about 33 — roughly a thousand-fold
drop, confirming the diagnosis rather than reflecting noise between runs.

But reward still didn't show a clean learning trend for either species, before
or after the fix. So fixing the critic's loss function fixed the instability,
but it didn't by itself produce a clear 'reward improves with training' story.

We didn't yet know if that was because this is a genuinely hard setup — 3v3,
predators three times slower than prey — or because the algorithm itself needed
more tuning. So we ran one more check."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 11 — 1v1 SANITY CHECK: FULL 2000-EPISODE RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"To separate 'hard task' from 'needs tuning,' we ran ActorCritic on a config
where we already have real DQN data to compare against directly: the 1v1 setup
with predator speed 2, prey speed 1, matching DQN's own 2000-episode horizon.

[Point to the DQN vs ActorCritic table]

DQN on this config shows a clear, monotonic trend — predator reward goes from
-970 in Q1 down to -2078 in Q4. That's the same co-evolutionary collapse we saw
in the original speed sweep, but it IS a trend — the Q-values are systematically
changing as both agents adapt.

ActorCritic's reward is flat across all four quarters, and starts already worse
than DQN's Q1. No trend up, no trend down. Just flat.

[Point to the capture rate table]

Here's the number that actually answers our question, though: capture rate.
Q1 it's 6.4%. Q4 it's 0.6%. That's not flat — that's a real decline, and we
checked it decile-by-decile to make sure it wasn't just quarter-level noise. It
holds up: 9% early, down to about 0.5% by the end.

The predator is getting WORSE at catching prey as training goes on. That
settles the question — this is a tuning issue, not just task difficulty.

Here's the subtle part: the critic's loss is dropping smoothly the whole time,
20 down to 0.33. In isolation that looks like convergence. But combined with
capture rate collapsing to near zero, it's actually a red flag — the critic is
learning to predict a boring, predictable outcome, because the policy has
stopped trying to do anything interesting. No captures means no surprises,
which means low loss, even while the actual behavior is getting worse."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 12 — DIAGNOSIS AND NEXT STEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Our diagnosis: entropy_coef is set to zero, so the policy has nothing pushing
back against this collapse. Look at DQN's own config for this exact scenario —
it deliberately slows epsilon decay so both agents keep exploring for almost
the entire 2000-episode run. That's a documented, deliberate choice that made
DQN work here. ActorCritic has no equivalent right now.

So the next step is a one-line, isolated change: raise entropy_coef from 0 to
0.01 — the standard default in the A2C and A3C literature — and re-run the
identical config, same seed, nothing else changed. If that prevents the
capture-rate collapse, that confirms the diagnosis and gives us clean
before/after evidence."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 13 — ENTROPY FIX: CONFIRMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"We ran it. Same config, same seed, one parameter changed.

[Point to the before/after capture rate table]

Before: capture rate collapses from 6.4% down to 0.6%. After: it holds steady
at 35 to 36 percent across all four quarters. We checked it decile by decile
too — 33.5 to 37.5 percent throughout, no late-training decay anywhere in the
run. Overall capture rate went from 4 percent to 35.4 percent. Roughly a
nine-times improvement, and the collapse is just gone.

That confirms the diagnosis. One parameter, entropy_coef, zero to 0.01, and the
predator stops forgetting how to hunt.

One thing we want to be upfront about rather than gloss over: predator reward
itself is still deeply negative and roughly flat, even with the much higher
capture rate. The accumulated distance-shaping penalty across the episodes that
still end in a timeout is dominating the reward number. So reward and actual
task performance are telling two different stories right now — capture rate is
the metric that actually shows what changed. That's a separate reward-shaping
question we still need to look at, not something this fix solved.

Our next steps: update the default config to entropy_coef 0.01 instead of 0,
since 0 is now a demonstrated-bad default, not just an untested one. Re-test
the harder 3v3 config with the same fix. Carry entropy regularization into A2C
from the start instead of rediscovering this. And separately dig into why
reward doesn't track capture rate the way we'd expect."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 14 — 3v3 RETEST: MIXED BUT HONEST RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"We updated the default config and re-ran the original 3-predator-vs-3-prey
setup with entropy_coef at 0.01.

[Point to the aggregate table]

The headline reward and loss numbers are basically unchanged from the entropy
0.0 run. Unlike 1v1, this fix doesn't visibly move the aggregate metrics here.

But we went back to the raw training log and counted actual captures by hand,
using episode length the same way we did for 1v1. And captures are happening
steadily through the whole run — roughly 16 to 28 percent of sampled episodes
across every quarter, no collapse. All three predators land captures at
different points, not just one agent carrying the team.

Here's the honest part: we don't have the same capture-count breakdown for the
original entropy 0.0 run on this config, because we only pulled reward and loss
aggregates from that one. So we can't give you a clean before-and-after capture
number for 3v3 the way we can for 1v1. We can tell you captures aren't
collapsing at 0.01. We can't tell you for certain that they would have
collapsed at 0.0.

So our conclusion, stated the way we actually mean it: the entropy fix is
cleanly validated on 1v1, with a dramatic, well-evidenced before and after. On
3v3, it's not hurting, and captures look healthy — but we're not going to claim
it's confirmed to help 3v3 specifically, because that's not what we actually
measured. Directionally consistent, not conclusively confirmed."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 17 — A2C: CRITIC LOSS, CONFIRMED AND FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"The prediction held. Predator critic loss sat at 262,519 down to 191,375
across quarters — hundreds of thousands, same magnitude and same
non-shrinking pattern as ActorCritic's pre-fix numbers. Same root cause, same
fix: swapped plain MSE for Huber loss.

[Point to the before/after table]

Before: 262,519 to 191,375. After: 144 to 154. That's about a 1,500-times
reduction — even bigger than ActorCritic's own fix. Capture rate came out
slightly higher too, 22.8 percent versus 18.8 before, and more stable across
quarters. A saner critic gives the actor cleaner gradient signal.

We also brought her console logging in line with ours for consistency, but
kept her extra metrics — she tracks actor loss and policy entropy separately,
which ActorCritic doesn't, and that turned out to matter for what we found
next."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 18 — A2C: ENTROPY COLLAPSE, INVESTIGATED BUT NOT SOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"The same run surfaced something more severe than the critic loss. Predator
entropy collapses to essentially zero by the second quarter — 0.149 down to
0.004, 0.004, 0.001 — despite entropy_coef already being set to 0.01 from the
start. That's the same value that held ActorCritic's capture rate stable at 35
percent. Here it doesn't hold at all. We checked her actual loss formula
directly, line by line — it's mechanically correct, same sign convention as
ours, no bug.

So we tried the obvious next step: raise entropy_coef five times, to 0.05,
same config, same seed. And the entropy metric still collapses to zero by Q3
and Q4. Raising the coefficient does not prevent it. That tells us this is
probably a structural training-dynamics issue — something like the policy's
logits growing large enough that the softmax saturates, at which point the
entropy bonus's weight barely matters anymore — not simply 'the number was too
small.'

[Point to the capture rate table]

Here's the twist, though: capture rate improved substantially anyway. 22.8
percent at entropy_coef 0.01, roughly 30 percent at 0.05 — consistently higher
across every quarter, even though the entropy metric itself collapsed either
way. So we raised the default to 0.05 in the real config, because it
measurably helps regardless of whether it fixes the thing we thought it would
fix. The actual root cause of the collapse is still an open question — we're
documenting it clearly rather than either hiding it or pretending we solved
it, and leaving it for whoever picks up this thread next."
